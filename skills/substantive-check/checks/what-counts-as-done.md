# Criterion 4 — What counts as done

Did you change the finish line? The acceptance criteria, exit
condition, definition of done, success measure or "until" that says
when work, a card, a PR, a curia question or a trial is complete. The
standing orders' screen asks this third: "the criterion for done". A
finish line moved passes work the old one failed, or fails work it
passed, and every agent under it aims at the new line without knowing
the old one moved.

Lineage: 10 CFR 50.59(c)(2)(vii), a design basis limit altered; here
the limit is the line the work must reach. Criterion 8 asks about the
ruler; this one asks about the mark on it the work is held to. A change
that fits both gets a row under each.

## What a done criterion is here

"Done when"; acceptance criteria on an issue or card; a card's `until:`;
a trial's end condition and what it must show; a PR's merge
requirements in prose; a spec row's pass condition; a scoping item's
deliverable; a hand-off prompt's verify step; a curia's `status:
settled` condition; a success metric or North Star; a checklist a
process must complete. The list is examples, never a fence.

## Detectors

- **An acceptance criterion added, dropped or reworded past its
  meaning** — the new words pass work the old would fail, or fail work
  the old would pass. `read`, both wordings quoted, the case named.
- **An exit condition moved** — a trial, a loop, a batch, a sitting or
  a cap now ends on a different condition or count. `git`.
- **A deliverable changed** — what must exist at the end: a test, a
  report, a spec row, a ledger line, a declaration in the PR body, now
  required or no longer. `git`.
- **A verify step added, dropped or weakened** — "done and verified"
  now means a different proof, or none. `read`.
- **An "until" moved** — a date, a count, a condition a decision or a
  default holds until. `git`.

A criterion restated, moved or given an example, where the same work
passes and the same work fails, is form.

## Declared looks like

A line under `## Substantive:` naming the file, `substantive (4)`, and
the finish line that moved: old beside new, and one piece of work that
would now pass or fail differently.

## `mu` when

The target states no finish line and is held to none that changed.
